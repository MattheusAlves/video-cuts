# Performance observations, 26 November 2026

These are single-machine observations, not a statistical benchmark. The source is the cached 19-minute Portuguese video `KXuTmIJmEAg`. After watching the two isolated end-to-end test drafts, the user confirmed that the cuts, captions, and framing were usable.

- Transcription: prior CPU `small` run took approximately 320 seconds including unmeasured surrounding work. Whisper CUDA was not tested: WSL can see the RTX 3060 Ti (8 GiB) but has no CUDA compiler installed.
- Selection: a GLM 5.3 Flash full scan of ten windows completed in 94 seconds with one candidate approved by the previous review logic. A later fast scan resumed four cached windows and obtained two candidates with explicit positive story and hook judgments in 5 seconds; that number is a **warm checkpoint retry**, not a cold full scan. The earlier failed attempt took about 37 seconds and was stopped by an incomplete review response. Review retry and unique, literal boundary repair have since been added.
- Rendering: CPU `libx264` produced 57.00- and 51.85-second 1080×1920 cuts in 20.854 and 16.388 seconds, respectively. On the first cut, an NVENC `p4`, VBR CQ 25 trial using the same video, filters, captions and audio took 9.329 seconds and produced 26,132,515 bytes; the CPU cut was 23,146,494 bytes. A second run through the actual pipeline with the new encoder option took 9.073 seconds and produced the same-sized output.
- A separate cold-selection, warm-media/transcript studio job processed five windows, performed three editorial review calls and rendered two cuts in 101.47 seconds: selection 55.71 seconds and CPU renders 17.40 and 18.07 seconds. Eight provider calls reported 13,261 input tokens, 5,014 output tokens and approximately $0.00376. This is one end-to-end sample, not an estimate for a fresh download or transcription. The user reviewed its two cuts and said the cuts, captions and framing were usable.
- At one frame per second, after scaling both outputs to 540×960, SSIM between the NVENC CQ 25 and CPU outputs was 0.9941. This compares two compressed outputs, not either to an uncompressed ground truth, and does not establish equivalent visual quality, caption accuracy or audio quality. Hardware, software versions and system load can affect results.
- A faster NVENC CQ 20 trial took 9.260 seconds but was 50,577,361 bytes. CPU remains the default; `clip.video_encoder: h264_nvenc` is opt-in until users review the output across more sources.

For four fixed selector windows, three serial trials took 21, 19 and 29 seconds; three trials with two requests in flight took 10, 10 and 11 seconds. All 24 requests returned parseable candidate JSON without an observed rate-limit error. Offline validation passed 1–2 candidates per serial run and 1–3 per concurrent run; this is not a human or editorial quality comparison. A later full fast scan completed in 26 seconds with four fresh selection windows and two approved candidates at concurrency two; a separate serial run took 92 seconds with nine windows. The difference includes stochastic selection and different numbers of windows, so it is not an isolated speedup estimate. Concurrency remains opt-in at two and defaults to one. See [OpenRouter's rate-limit guidance](https://openrouter.ai/pricing); provider-specific limits and charges still apply.

The compact selector format and larger windows have not passed a reliable end-to-end quality comparison; keep them experimental. The original scan was cancelled before its full baseline could be measured. The new reviewable outputs are in `clips/pending/` within the worktree, which is ignored by Git.
